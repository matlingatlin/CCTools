Scio — three sketches

Two directions were on the table: an owl in the O, or a working factory with turning wheels. The third sketch is the argument that they are the same drawing.

Direction 1

The owl in the O

The literal reading of scio — “I know”. The O is a face: two eyes, a beak, two tufts. It is quiet, it reads at 16 px, and it says knowledge and nothing else. The cost is that it says nothing about building, and every second AI company has an animal.

SCI

Moving part: the pupils. Nothing else. An owl that spins is a cartoon.

Direction 2

The working factory

A gear train and two cylinders, actually turning. It says this thing builds, which is the promise. The cost is that a gear is the most-used icon in software, and mechanical detail disappears below about 32 px.

Moving parts: one gear and two cylinders, out of phase. (The earlier caption claimed a gear train; the file has one gear, and the second “gear” was a circle rotating about its own centre — a visual no-op. Claim removed rather than kept.)

Direction 3 — the recommendation

The gear is the O, and the owl is inside it

A gear seen head-on is an O. So the two directions were never a choice. The teeth turn; the face does not. That is the product in one drawing: the machinery works, and the thing that knows sits still and watches. Drop the teeth below 24 px and the owl survives alone as a favicon.

SCI

The part that matters more than the drawing

Animation should mean something, or it should not run

A logo that spins forever is decoration, and decoration is what everyone else ships. If the mark turns only when work is happening, it becomes the cheapest honest signal in the product — and this system’s whole diagnosed failure is that it computes honest signals and drops them before anyone sees them.
